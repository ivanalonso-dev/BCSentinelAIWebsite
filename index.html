Method

Formation happens in three moments. We're built to detect the first one.

Markets price what's visible. By the time a launch, a listing, or a disclosure makes a formation public, it has usually been assembling on-chain for weeks or months. That's the window we detect in — before the market has priced it.

T1 · FIRST SIGNAL

A pattern starts

A multisig Safe gets deployed, a role gets granted, funding starts concentrating from a small number of sources. Individually, any one of these is noise.

T2 · ALERT THRESHOLD

The pattern corroborates

Enough independent signals line up — deployment timing, funding concentration, entity age — that the case crosses our internal threshold for human review.

T3 · PUBLIC VISIBILITY

The entity confirms itself

A launch, a listing, a press mention, a verified contract. This is where most tools start looking. We measure how far ahead of this point we actually were.

Every classification we produce has to trace back to a specific, observable piece of on-chain evidence. If we can't point to the transaction, the contract, or the timing that justifies a score, we don't assign one.

Our engines are built to say outright when they don't have a historical basis for estimating a probability — rather than estimate one anyway.

investigate_wallet — core classification engine

Now in its seventh revision. Version 3 closed the blind spot that mattered most: a sophisticated actor operating through a Gnosis Safe showed up only as execTransaction(...) — the real action hidden inside.

  • Safe-payload decoding — recovers the real selector inside every Safe transaction
  • MultiSend decoding — unpacks every batched sub-transaction independently
  • Economic-value extraction — converts raw transfer data into readable amounts
  • Contract-creation check — catches deployments with no destination address, including EIP-1167 minimal-proxy clones

Every wallet in our calibration set carries a direct, timestamped investigate_wallet output file — the pipeline's own evidence trail, generated as it runs, not a label applied after the fact.

Scope today: Ethereum mainnet (Layer 1) — 187 formations investigated in depth, with live detection already running against our calibrated signal set.

Confidence Engine — evidence reliability

A separate engine from the classifier above: this one scores how much to trust the evidence itself, not the size of the formation. Five independent categories, non-linear gating between them, and an explicit, severity-weighted penalty for contradictions — with full provenance kept on every input that fed the score.

Fraud Risk Engine — outcome classification

Classifies what actually happened to a formation after the fact: active, legitimate wind-down, confirmed exit scam, slow insider extraction, or silent shutdown. Its silence-pattern detector was validated against a real historical case before being trusted on the rest of the dataset.

Organization-first prefilter — TS-002 / TS-002B

Runs ahead of individual-wallet investigation, scanning for organizational signatures across a wider net before a candidate ever reaches the classifier. Every prefilter carries an explicit recency window, so a candidate that goes quiet doesn't sit in the queue as a false positive indefinitely.

Verification

We don't ask you to take our word for it.

Two independent mechanisms back every case we track — one checks the claim against public sources, the other proves we didn't rewrite our own history.

Cross-source confirmation

Every candidate we track gets checked against GitHub code search, Etherscan's verified-contract registry, and DefiLlama's protocol index — three independent, public sources outside our control. A case only advances when the on-chain pattern lines up with something checkable off-chain.

Cryptographic sealing

Starting this cycle, every new formation we begin tracking is sealed the moment we see it — a hash of the case record, chained to the one sealed before it, with a timestamp. If that formation becomes public later, anyone can recompute the hash and confirm it matches what we sealed.
